You get the reasoning and the sources, not just a verdict.

How it works

A claim goes in, six stages run, a score and a confidence come out. Nothing is answered from model memory — every judgement is made against text we fetched for that specific claim. The three marked stages are where the work is ours: retrieval is a commodity anyone can rent, but adjudicating conflicting evidence and knowing what the answer is worth is not.

1 · Structure

The claim is rewritten as one precise, falsifiable sentence and broken into the atomic assertions that must all hold. We also classify it — factual, statistical, causal, predictive or opinion — because that determines how far evidence can take us.

2 · Query the live web, in parallel

We generate several different search angles for the same claim, including one written specifically to surface contradicting evidence, then crawl and read the open web across four independent retrieval lanes at once — a grounded-answer lane, a semantic lane, a live search index and a deep-research lane. They run simultaneously, so breadth costs seconds, not minutes. Then the claim's type decides what else gets asked: a claim about the future also goes to live prediction markets, where a share price is a probability with real money behind it, and a claim about cause-and-effect or population statistics goes to the peer-reviewed literature, weighted by how much the field actually cites each study. Firing every source at every claim is slower and noisier — knowing which authority settles which kind of question is the point.

3 · Judge each lane on its own evidenceDefensible

Every lane is scored in isolation, strictly against the pages that lane actually fetched, with background knowledge explicitly forbidden. Each judge reports a stance, a probability, and how decisive its evidence really is — and must name which sources it leaned on, so weak or off-topic pages stop inflating the source count.

4 · Aggregate, and let disagreement cost youDefensible

Lanes are combined weighted by how decisive each one was. When independent lanes land on opposite sides, confidence drops — that spread is the honest signal that the question is open. A lane that came back empty stays in the denominator, so silence lowers the score instead of quietly disappearing.

5 · Calibration reviewDefensible

A final adversarial pass asks whether the verdict is really carried by its evidence, or only by evidence that is adjacent to the claim. It is a one-way ratchet: it can lower confidence, never raise it. Claims about the future or matters of opinion are capped here — absence of reporting is not proof of absence.

6 · Score, and keep watching

The output is a truth score from 0 to 100 — above 50 leans true — plus a separate confidence saying how much to trust that score at all, and the sources behind it. Claims that aren't settled today can be pinned and re-checked daily, so you see the score move as reality does.

“But a model is still doing the judging — why trust that?”

The fair objection, and the reason the architecture looks the way it does. Asking a model to recall a fact is where hallucination lives: it answers from compressed memory with no way to know whether it knows. Asking it whether this specific text supports this specific claim is reading comprehension over material placed in front of it — a far more reliable task, and one you can audit yourself by opening the sources. On top of that we never rely on a single read: several lanes see different evidence, each conclusion is attacked before it is accepted, and disagreement between them lowers the score automatically. The confidence number is the part we are hardening next — fitting it against outcomes we have observed, rather than asking a model how sure it feels.

Every check is logged with its full reasoning trace, which becomes the training corpus for improving the judging over time.

Connect any LLM agent

This whole check is exposed as one MCP tool — verify_claim. Any agent (Claude, Cursor, your own) can call it and get back the verdict, the confidence, the per-lane breakdown and the sources. Access is key-gated: it only answers to a key we issue.
